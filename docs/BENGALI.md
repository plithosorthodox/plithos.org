# Bengali: the register, settled before the writing starts

Bengali is the first of the three saint kinds this site has ever written in
Bengali: no vocabulary, no lives, no calendar entries. What it does have is
four bodies of published Bengali, and every rule below is read off them with
the count given, so that a later reader can check the arithmetic instead of
taking the ruling on trust. Nothing here is settled by ear.

| file | what it settles | size |
|---|---|---|
| `data/prayers-i18n.v2.bn.json` | the liturgical register and the divine names | 175,240 characters |
| `data/saint-names.v1.bn.json` | 1,528 commemorations - every rank word and the word order | |
| `data/glossary-i18n.v1.bn.json` | 177 ecclesiastical terms, defined | |
| `data/saint-info.v1.bn.json` | 119 calendar entries in modern prose | |

**Where they disagree the prayers win**, because they are the Church's own
books. They are silent about ranks, since a prayer names no saint by his
order, and there the names table decides: it is the largest body, it is
specifically about how a saint is named, and this vocabulary stands beside it
on the same card.

## The two hazards

Bengali Christian vocabulary is overwhelmingly Protestant and Roman Catholic,
and a dictionary or a concordance hands it over fluently. That is the first
hazard and it is the ordinary one.

The second is particular to Bengali. The language draws its high register from
Sanskrit, and a word can be exact and still carry a settled Hindu sense that
makes it wrong in a Christian index. **সাধু** is the clearest case: it is
good Bengali for a holy man, it is what a dictionary offers for *saint*, and
it is what a Bengali reader hears as a Hindu ascetic. The prayers write
**সন্ত** 36 times against 13, and the names table writes সন্ত 389 times and
সাধু not once. The count decides, and it decides the same way twice.

| saint | সন্ত | 36 | সাধু 13 |

**খ্রীষ্ট and not খ্রিস্ট.** Both spellings are in use in Bengali Christian
printing; this site has settled on the first, 147 to nothing, and a single
খ্রিস্ট in this table would be a second usage rather than a variant.

**The Theotokos.** The prayers say ঈশ্বরের মাতা and the names table writes the
compound **ঈশ্বরমাতা** 36 times in the genitive. They are the same word and
the difference is grammatical: the phrase standing alone is ঈশ্বরের মাতা, and
before another noun it is ঈশ্বরমাতার - ঈশ্বরমাতার আইকন, the icon of the
Mother of God. The glossary's থেওতোকোস is left where it is, in the glossary,
explaining the Greek word; it does not stand in a saint's name.

## Which register

**Cholit-bhasha**, the modern standard, in the elevated tone the prayers keep.
This is a count and not a preference: across the hundred prayers the sadhu
forms do not appear at all.

    থেকে 333    হইতে 0
    করে  396    করিয়া 0
    তাঁর 116    তাঁহার 0

God and the saints are addressed as **তুমি** - তোমার 1006 against আপনার 102 -
which is the liturgical second person and not familiarity. The vocabulary is
not addressed to anyone, so the question rarely arises in it; where a phrase
does address the saint, তুমি is what the prayers use.

Tatsama vocabulary is used where the Church already uses it - অলৌকিককর্মী,
স্বীকারকারী, ধ্বংসাবশেষ - and is not reached for beyond that. A phrase in this
table stands under a life written in the same modern Bengali, and a Sanskritic
label over a modern paragraph reads as a quotation.

## How a saint's name is written

Read off the 1,528 commemorations, which have already done this work.

**The scriptural saints take the Bengali biblical forms**, which are what
Bengali Christians of every confession read:

    যোহন 46 (against জন 16)    পৌল 14        পিতর 8
    স্তেফান 8                   মীখায়েল 7     মরিয়ম

**The saints of the later Church are transcribed as the names table already
transcribes them**, which is through the shape English gives the name rather
than through the Greek: নিকোলাস, জর্জ, গ্রেগরি, বেসিল (12 against বাসিল 4).
This is not what Georgian or Japanese do, and it is stated plainly here rather
than corrected: there is no printed Bengali Orthodox synaxarion to override the
table with, the table is the largest body of Bengali hagiographical naming that
exists on this site, and a vocabulary that spelled the same saint one way while
the card beside it spelled him another would be a defect a reader sees at once.
Where the table has a saint, its spelling stands.

Bengali script throughout. No Latin letters inside a Bengali phrase, no Devanagari,
European digits as the rest of the site uses, hyphens rather than dashes, and
straight quotes.

**প্রেরিততুল্য for equal-to-the-apostles**, nine against eight, because the
compound is the shorter of the two and these are labels standing in a column.

**বিশপ, আর্চবিশপ, প্যাট্রিয়ার্ক and মেট্রোপলিটন** are the table's words for the
offices, and they are kept although the glossary defines the same offices as
ধর্মাধ্যক্ষ, পিতৃপ্রধান and মিত্রোপলিত. The glossary is explaining the words to
a reader; the names table is naming saints, which is what this vocabulary does,
and the two hundred and fifty-four instances there are the usage.

**A monastic is শ্রদ্ধেয়, not merely সন্ত.** This is the distinction English
does not make and the one most often lost. The names table gives it to the
monastic saints 354 times, and `tools/check_register.py --lang bn` asserts it
and nothing else. Bengali lets সন্ত stand before a bare name, as Greek and
Georgian let their word stand, so `strict` is False.

## Word order

Bengali builds the noun phrase head-last and puts the place first, in the
genitive, where English puts it last in a prepositional phrase. The names table
already does this and it is the thing a rendering gives itself away by:

    Saint Nicholas the Wonderworker, Archbishop of Myra in Lycia
      লিসিয়ার মীরার সন্ত অলৌকিককর্মী নিকোলাস, আর্চবিশপ

    Venerable Sergius of Radonezh
      রাদোনেজের শ্রদ্ধেয় সের্গিয়ুস

    of the Kyiv Caves
      কিয়েভ গুহার

An entry that keeps the English order - the rank, then a Bengali rendering of
"of X" trailing behind it - is legible and immediately foreign, which is the
whole defect this register exists to prevent.

## The fields, and what each one is

The queue is alphabetical by the English phrase and mixes the fields together,
so knowing which field a phrase came from settles most of the questions about
how to render it.

| field | count | what it is | how Bengali takes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| patronCauses | 2,622 | what is asked of him | a noun phrase: রোগী ও দরিদ্রদের জন্য |
| patronWork | 1,572 | whom he is asked for | likewise |
| related | 1,476 | the kindred commemoration | a name and its apposition |
| icon | 1,407 | how he is written in an icon | a full descriptive sentence, ending in a stop |
| titles | 980 | how else the saint is named | ইফিষের বিশপ, স্বীকারকারী |
| relics | 749 | where the relics rest | a full sentence, as the English is |
| place, origin, region, country | 1,207 | town, then the land it stood in | received form, comma as in the English |
| patronPlaces | 537 | the town a saint is patron of | the bare place name |
| rank, state, type | 208 | his order and his standing | a noun phrase |
| canonizedBy | 145 | who glorified him and when | a clause |
| baptismalName | 101 | the name before tonsure | the Bengali form of the name |
| era | 27 | the century | চতুর্থ শতাব্দী |

The icon lines are the long ones and they are sentences: they end with the
Bengali full stop, the দাঁড়ি **।**, as the prayers do. The noun phrases - the
places, the patronages, the titles - are not sentences and take no stop.

## A rank standing over the Caves is fronted

The English titles come in two shapes and the file answers them differently,
which looks like an inconsistency and is the file's own settled habit.

A rank followed by a place in the genitive - `the Recluse, of the Kyiv Far
Caves` - puts the place first, as the register requires:

    কিয়েভের দূরবর্তী গুহার নির্জনবাসী

A rank followed by a bare see or town keeps the comma the English has, because
that is what the twenty-odd Confessor entries already print:

    the Confessor, of Decapolis      স্বীকারকারী, দেকাপোলিসের

Both patterns stood in the file before this vocabulary began - the Ascetic and
the Canonarch of the Far Caves fronted, the Deacon of the Near Caves and the
Dry of the Near Caves did not - and the tie is broken by the register itself,
which says the place comes first. Fronting is used for the Caves throughout.

The Fathers and the Synaxis entries are the exception and are left alone: they
say কিয়েভ দূর গুহার পিতারা and দূর গুহার পিতাদের স্মরণসভা, not দূরবর্তী, because
that is how the four entries already written say it.

## Spellings settled while writing the vocabulary

| the word | written | against | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crete | ক্রিট | ক্রীট | the names table, 4 to 1, and the `of Crete` entry beside it |
| Cyrrhus | কুররোস | সিরাস | 4 to 2 in the file |
| the Horde | হোর্দ | হোর্ড | 5 to 1 |
| China | চীন | চিন | 36 to 13 |
| Recluse | নির্জনবাসী | নিভৃতবাসী | 148 to 5 across the file and the names table |
| David | দাউদ | দায়ূদ | 22 to 1 |

**David is the one that goes against the brief and is written down for that
reason.** The prayers say দায়ূদ once, the Bengali biblical form; the names
table and this vocabulary say দাউদ twenty-two times between them. The rule
that the prayers win governs the register and the divine names, and the rule
that the names table decides governs how a saint is spelled. This is a saint's
name, so the table decides, and a card that spelled the Prophet-King one way
in his rank and another in the commemoration beside it would be a defect a
reader sees at once.

Where nothing was published at all, the Bengali biblical form is taken:
মশীহ for the Messiah, হবক্কূক for Habakkuk, যিহোশূয় for Joshua, তিশবীয় for
the Tishbite. **Joseph is two names, not one.** The scriptural Joseph is
যোষেফ, as the names table and the prayers have him; the later saints - the
Hymnographer, Volokolamsk - are জোসেফ, as the transcription rule requires.

## Where the names table and the prayers disagree

The rule the brief gives is that the prayers win, and the names table decides
only the ranks and the spelling of names and places. One feast shows why. The
Exaltation of the Cross is উন্নয়ন in the names table (4) and উত্তোলন in the
prayers (1). উন্নয়ন in living Bengali means development, which is not what is
done to the Cross on the fourteenth of September; the prayers say উত্তোলন,
lifting up, and the prayers win. The file writes উত্তোলন.

## The order of work

    python3 tools/loop.py terms bn --start Bengali
    python3 tools/loop.py terms bn --next 40
    python3 tools/check_register.py --lang bn

`tools/saint_terms/bn.py` is written entirely as `TEXT`; `PARTS` and `expand()`
are optional and the builder calls `expand()` only if it exists. Deciding, for
each of ten thousand phrases, whether it is an atom or a compound is a judgement
per phrase, and a judgement per phrase is a stop per phrase. Write them out and
factor later, or never. See `docs/LOOP.md`.
